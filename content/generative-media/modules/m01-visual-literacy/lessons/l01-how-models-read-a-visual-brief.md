## Teaching

### The Monday-morning problem

At **Northstar Studio**, a teammate drops this into the image model:

> “Make a cool product shot for our launch.”

Sometimes you get a hero still on marble. Sometimes neon cyberpunk. Same model. Same brand. Different day—different vibe.

The model isn’t broken. **The brief left too many visual decisions open.** Clear visual briefs remove decisions the model shouldn’t own.

### Clear vs vague (side by side)

| Vague | Clear |
|-------|--------|
| Cool product shot | Northstar Studio desk lamp on walnut desk, 3/4 hero angle, soft window key light, shallow DOF, clean negative space for logo |
| Make it cinematic | 35mm look, anamorphic bokeh, teal-orange grade, motivated practical lamps |
| Fancy background | Soft gradient #0b0f19 → charcoal, subtle grain, no busy patterns |

Clarity is not “more adjectives.” Clarity is **named visual decisions**.

### Four signals of a shootable brief

1. **Subject is concrete** — what sits in frame, countable.
2. **Scene is grounded** — place, time-of-day, surface.
3. **Camera is specified** — angle, lens feel, depth of field.
4. **Style is locked** — lighting mood + look references (not “beautiful”).

### Talk-over narration (instructor VO)

*(Beat 1)* “If your still works once and fails the next day, you left visual decisions open.”

*(Beat 2)* “Subject, scene, camera, style—lock those four and Northstar’s desk lamp stops drifting into cyberpunk.”

*(Beat 3)* “You’re done when you can spot a mushy brief in ten seconds and name the missing lock.”

## Worked micro-example

**Before**

> Make a cool product shot for our launch.

**After**

> Northstar Studio brass desk lamp on a walnut desk, 3/4 hero angle, soft north-window key light with gentle rim, shallow depth of field, dark #0b0f19 negative space for logo lockup, photoreal product photography, no people.

Shootable briefs = **subject + scene + camera + style**. Everything else stacks on that base.
