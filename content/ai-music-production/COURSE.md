**Course ID:** `ai-music-production`  
**Tagline:** Write, generate, arrange, and ship tracks with AI music tools  
**Level:** Beginner · **Duration:** 6 hours · **Lessons:** 24  
**Pacing:** ~1 hour per curriculum module × 6 modules  
**Gradient:** `#ec4899` → `#8b5cf6`  
**Product:** The Ai Master Class

---

## Who this is for

Creators, producers, and marketers who want to write, generate, arrange, and ship AI-assisted music—without getting lost in one vendor’s UI or pasting copyrighted lyrics.

No coding required. Patterns work across Suno/Udio-style generators (model-agnostic). Scenarios use fictional **Northstar Studio** and `learner@example.com` only.

This course is **music only**. Image/video pipelines live in the separate `generative-media` course—do not overlap.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

1. Lock a **shippable song brief** with genre, mood, reference vibe, length, and taste locks.
2. Write **lyrics and structure** that land—hooks without cliché sludge.
3. **Generate and iterate** without losing the song (extend, vary, vocal vs instrumental).
4. Produce **arrangement maps and mix notes** models can follow.
5. Handle **rights, credits, metadata, and platform-safe** release copy.
6. Run a **weekly music OS** that ships one track.

## Prerequisites

- Access to any modern AI music generator (Suno/Udio-style or similar)
- A notes app for briefs, locks, and catalogs
- Optional: a DAW for light edits (habits transfer even without one)

### Module 1 — Song brief & taste lock *(built)*

1. What a shippable AI track needs  
2. Brief: genre, mood, reference, length  
3. Taste locks that survive regeneration  
4. Common music-prompt failure modes  

### Module 2 — Lyrics & structure *(built)*

1. Verse, chorus, bridge that land  
2. Hook writing without cliché sludge  
3. Rhyme, cadence, and singability  
4. Lyric rewrite loops  

### Module 3 — Generate & iterate *(built)*

1. First-pass generation that matches the brief  
2. Extend, cover, and variation without losing the song  
3. Vocals vs instrumental decisions  
4. When to regenerate vs edit  

### Module 4 — Arrangement & mix notes *(built)*

1. Arrangement map the model can follow  
2. Stem thinking without real stems  
3. Mix notes: loud, muddy, empty  
4. Reference matching  

### Module 5 — Rights, credits, release *(built)*

1. What you can claim you made  
2. Credits, metadata, and cover-art brief  
3. Platform-safe descriptions  
4. A one-track release checklist  

### Module 6 — A weekly music OS *(built)*

1. Batch writing sessions  
2. Cataloging reusable music prompts  
3. Handoff to a video/image teammate  
4. Ship one track this week
