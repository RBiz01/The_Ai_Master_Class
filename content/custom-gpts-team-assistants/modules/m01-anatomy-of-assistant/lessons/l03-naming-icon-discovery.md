**Module:** Anatomy of a custom assistant

## Learning objective

Write a name, one-line description, and icon brief that set correct expectations—so teammates pick the right assistant.

## What to do

1. Watch the **Lesson video** once for the visual hook.
2. Read **Teaching** (and the worked example) without rushing.
3. Open `l03-interactive.html` and follow its Instructions until you hit the success state (this locks in: Naming, icon & discovery cues).
4. Rewrite or apply the idea once in your own words (one sentence is enough).
5. **You’re done when:** you can explain — in plain language — *Write a name, one-line description, and icon brief that set correct expectations—so teammates pick the right assistant.* and `l03-interactive.html` shows success.

## Teaching

### Beat 1

Bad names ('Helper Bot', 'AI Thing') create wrong expectations and low trust.

### Beat 2

Good names encode job + audience: Acme Ops Vendor Triage, Portal Go-Live Coach, Invoice Exception Drafter.

### Beat 3

Icon and description are UX: promise the job, not the model brand. Avoid fake logos of real AI vendors.

### Talk-over narration (instructor VO)

*(Beat 1)*  
“At Acme Ops, assistants only work when teammates trust them. This lesson locks that trust.”

*(Beat 2)*  
“Stay model-agnostic: the same pattern applies whether you ship a Custom GPT, a Project, or a Gem.”

*(Beat 3)*  
“You’re done when you can apply this on a fictional Acme Ops workflow without guessing.”

## Worked micro-example

**Before**

> Name: SuperGPT. Description: Answers anything about work.

**After**

> Name: Acme Ops Vendor Triage. Description: Ranks vendor emails for standup; drafts replies; never sends. Icon brief: purple checklist with cyan envelope, no brand logos.
