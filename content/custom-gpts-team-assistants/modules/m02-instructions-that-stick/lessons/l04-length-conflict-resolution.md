# Lesson 2.4 — Length & conflict resolution

**Module:** Instructions that stick  
**Duration:** ~6–8 min teaching + ~4–8 min interactive  
**Interactive:** `l04-interactive.html`

---

## Learning objective

Keep instructions short enough to follow, resolve conflicts deliberately, and link long policy to knowledge instead of pasting it.

## What to do

1. Play the **Lesson video** all the way through — treat it as the cold open.
2. Read **Teaching** and the worked micro-example like a playbook, not a skim.
3. Open `l04-interactive.html` and follow its Instructions until you hit the success state (this locks in: Length & conflict resolution).
4. Capture a one-sentence Monday-morning takeaway you could paste into Slack.
5. **You’re done when:** you can explain — in plain language — *Keep instructions short enough to follow, resolve conflicts deliberately, and link long policy to knowledge instead of pasting it.* and `l04-interactive.html` shows success.

## Teaching

### Beat 1

Long instructions conflict with themselves. Prefer 400–900 words of sharp rules over a pasted handbook.

### Beat 2

When two rules fight, write a precedence line: safety > accuracy > brevity > style.

### Beat 3

Move examples and long policies into knowledge; keep the contract in instructions.

### Talk-over narration (instructor VO)

*(Beat 1)*  
“At Acme Ops, assistants only work when teammates trust them. This lesson locks that trust.”

*(Beat 2)*  
“Stay model-agnostic: the same pattern applies whether you ship a Custom GPT, a Project, or a Gem.”

*(Beat 3)*  
“You’re done when you can apply this on a fictional Acme Ops workflow without guessing.”

## Worked micro-example

**Before**

> 12 pages of pasted handbook plus 'be brief' and 'include every edge case'.

**After**

> Precedence: safety > accuracy > brevity. Handbook in knowledge. Instructions: 1 page contract + 2 exemplars max.
