# Lesson 3.1 — Knowledge vs the prompt

**Module:** Files, knowledge & boundaries  
**Duration:** ~6–8 min teaching + ~4–8 min interactive  
**Interactive:** `l01-interactive.html`

## Learning objective

Split durable reference material into knowledge and keep the behavior contract in instructions.

## What to do

1. Watch the **Lesson video** once; pause only if a beat truly clicks for your role.
2. Work through **Teaching** and the worked example until you could explain it out loud.
3. Open `l01-interactive.html` and follow its Instructions until you hit the success state (this locks in: Knowledge vs the prompt).
4. Jot one reusable line in your own words — something you’d actually use at work.
5. **You’re done when:** you can explain — in plain language — *Split durable reference material into knowledge and keep the behavior contract in instructions.* and `l01-interactive.html` shows success.

## Teaching

### Beat 1

Instructions: how to behave. Knowledge: what to consult (policies, glossaries, SOPs, FAQs).

### Beat 2

If a fact changes monthly, it belongs in a versioned knowledge file—not frozen in instructions.

### Beat 3

Acme Ops: SLA table and vendor playbook → knowledge. 'Never invent SLAs' → instructions.

### Talk-over narration (instructor VO)

*(Beat 1)*  
“At Acme Ops, assistants only work when teammates trust them. This lesson locks that trust.”

*(Beat 2)*  
“Stay model-agnostic: the same pattern applies whether you ship a Custom GPT, a Project, or a Gem.”

*(Beat 3)*  
“You’re done when you can apply this on a fictional Acme Ops workflow without guessing.”
